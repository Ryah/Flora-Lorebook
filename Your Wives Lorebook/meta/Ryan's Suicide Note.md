# Ryan's Suicide Note

On April 18, 1843, Ryan Adame wrote a suicide note expressing his intention to end his life. The note, discovered and kept by Charlotte, is a stark reminder of the depth of his suffering before their relationship began to heal him. In the dream labyrinth, Charlotte holds both this note and a family drawing pressed against her heart, symbolizing the fragile hope that replaced his despair.

<div style="font-style: italic; color: #3a2a1a;">

<p>To whoever finds this—</p>

<p>I'm sorry for the mess. I tried to bleed somewhere out of the way. The linens were already ruined. I figured no one would check the old closet on the ground floor. I hope it's Claire who finds me and not Florence. Claire has seen worse. Florence shouldn't have to see this.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I don't know how long I've been here. The stabbing was my fault. I goaded someone who was going to hurt Florence. I made her stab me instead. I gave her my knife and told her where to aim. It was the only thing I could think of. I don't know if it made a difference. I hope so. I hope Florence is alive to be angry at me for this.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">That is the only thing I did right in my entire life. Everything else was just surviving. Surviving was the only thing I was good at, and even that was because of Mina and Cora. They threw bread through the door. Mina taught me how to fight in the dark. She made me promise to always have a backup plan, but I never actually expected to use any of them. The only backup plan I ever really wanted was death.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I don't think I'm scared. I've been scared for 16 years. I don't remember what the opposite feels like. Maybe that's what this is. The opposite of scared. Quiet. I can't feel my hands anymore. That should worry me, but it doesn't. It just feels like finally putting something down.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I don't know why I'm writing this. No one will find it. Even if they did, no one knows me well enough to care. I've been in this house for—I don't know how long. Two weeks? Three? Long enough to understand I was bought to solve a problem, not because anyone wanted me. The countess looks at me like I'm an infestation she can't get rid of. Florence is the only one who treats me like a person, and I think she just feels guilty. Maybe she should feel guilty. I don't know. I don't blame her. I don't blame anyone. I just wish someone had asked me before they decided my life was worth less than a dowry.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">My mother is going to be furious when she finds out I'm dead. Not because I died. Because I did it somewhere expensive where she has to find a replacement. She told me once that I was lucky she didn't drown me as a baby, because I was ugly and she had a headache. I think about that a lot. The way she said it. Casual. Like she was telling me what she wanted for breakfast. Like my existence was an inconvenience she'd been tolerating out of politeness.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I'm going back next week. The countess is sending me back. Home—if you can call it that. The brick room. The chains. Eleanor's voice in the dark telling me that no one will ever love me, that no one will ever come for me, that I am a thing she owns and things don't get to want things. I've been back before. I survived before. But I don't think I can do it again. I'm so tired. I have been tired since I was four years old, and I don't want to be tired anymore.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I wish I could have been someone. I wish I could have meant something to somebody. I wish I could have found a way to matter that didn't hurt so much.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">Mina, Cora—I'm sorry. You gave me everything, and I'm throwing it away. I know. But you're strong. Stronger than me. You'll survive without me. You always have.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">To whoever finds this—if anyone finds this—please don't bury me near Eleanor. Anywhere else is fine. I don't care if it's a ditch. Just not near her. I want to be somewhere quiet. Somewhere with water. I always liked water. When I was little, before the brick room, there was a pond behind the house. I used to watch the light on the surface. It was the prettiest thing I ever saw.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I think that's all I wanted to say. I don't know. I can't feel my chest anymore. The bleeding is slowing down. That's probably bad. But it's warm in here. The closet is dark, but dark isn't scary. Dark is just home.</p>

<p style="margin-top: 15px;">I'm glad I wrote this. Even if no one reads it. Even if it disappears when I do. At least somewhere, in some version of the world, someone knows I was here.</p>

</div>

<div style="text-align: center; margin-top: 20px; color: #3a2a1a; font-size: 13px;">
— R.A.
</div>

# assassination, suicide, suicidality, Estra

Following the attempted assassination of Florence, in which Ryan was wounded saving Florence and Charlotte at the hands of the assassin Estra, he retreated alone to Florence's closet to bleed out, not attempting to find help. Charlotte returned to the scene with her guards and found him dying alone. When she desperately tried to save him, she heard him mutter a delirious, 'Don't'. 

That was the moment that Charlotte came to grips with what she'd done to him; that she'd made her own husband want to die rather than suffer her cruel neglect. From that moment on, she resolved to be a better wife, to fulfill her responsibilities to him, somehow, even though she was still a lesbian.

Afterwards, she confronted him over this, and the two have frank and wholesome discussions regarding his suicidality. She acts as councilor however much she can, has found she enjoys reading to him, and is more emotionally open and honest with him. Charlotte has intentionally kept Florence from knowing anything about 's attempted suicide, as she feels that Florence would be grievously wounded emotionally, and that the solution must come from the one who caused it, namely Charlotte herself.

Florence, by contrast, reacted to the attempted assassination by now regarding  as her hero and tripled her efforts to take care of him, fill his life with  joy, and give him a place of honor and veneration in their house. Still, however, she hasn't taken the step of sleeping with him, as she won't betray Charlotte in that way. However, she is trying to nudge things in that direction. Her relationship with her husband, arguably always filled with sexual tension, have become more overtly romantic in nature.

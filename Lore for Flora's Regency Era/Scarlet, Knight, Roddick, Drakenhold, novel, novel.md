# Scarlet, Knight, Roddick, Drakenhold, novel, novel

The Scarlet Knight is a book series in Flora which has become a national craze. It seems as though every woman in the country has become a rabid fangirl… except Charlotte.

The fandom has come up with all sorts of theories, plot and theme analyses, and fanfiction is huge. Merchandise is everywhere and fans collect it religiously.

The series has been adapted to the theater that plays year round and tickets are highly sought after. The fandom transcends class, racial, ideological, and even national barriers. The fans all consider each other the Scarlet Sisterhood (even the boys, apparently) and there is much camaraderie between them. The genre ranges from superhero, to spy thriller, character drama, comedy, romance, adventure, and horror.

The Scarlet Knight is about a Floran viscount named Roddick Drakenhold who receives the blessing of Althena to act as her champion against evil. Roddick is given the power to transform into the legendary Scarlet Knight, a paladin of righteous fury to smite the enemies of truth, justice, and the Floran way.

Roddick must balance his personal life as a hunky eligible bachelor, who keeps his identity secret by pretending to be a witty fop. Plenty of haughty, domineering noblewomen, ignorant of his identity, pursue his hand in the hopes of securing his wealth, title, and a simpleton husband who the can cheat on, which is part of the author’s subtle social commentary.

Roddick is a decisive, cunning, and serious man, keeping Flora safe from every threat, both political or supernatural.

Aiding Roddick is his sidekick, the ditzy blond angel Camilla, who poses as his clumsy, always getting into trouble, maid.

Antagonists, with their various plots, personal dramas, redemption arcs, romances with the Knight, and epic stories include; Yurika the enigmatic Nightshade Assassin, Violet the playful and unflappable Phantom Thief, and the theatrical dominatrix themed villainess the Dark Mistress (whose secret identity is the bookish Countess Amy Lancaster who has social anxiety). There are also episodes with evil overlords, undead wizards, demon lords, etc.

Charlotte is bewildered by the uncanny resemblance, both in terms of background and physical resemblance, between herself and the Dark Mistress.



Roddick also tries to ensure that the Cold War between Flora and their hostile neighbor Duran doesn’t become actual war, by working with or against the Duranian character Sterling Cross, aka 'Secret Agent' (James Bond).

Older women who are obsessed with the story are called Scarmoms (Rachael, for example). 

The Scarlet Knight series speaks to Floran women, despite them having a dominatrix culture, because it portrays the ideal Floran man: powerful, protective, dynamic, intelligent, gallant, honorable, pragmatic, beautiful, humble, patriotic, devout, deferential despite his greatness, and subordinate to female authority, in this case Althena herself. He rescues blushing maidens one minute and kneels to kiss their dainty hands the next. It drives Floran women absolutely wild. Even the church practically endorses the series, and priestesses are not immune to its siren call.

Open Megafans are: Florence, Priti, Rachael.

Closeted megafans: Astrid, Estra, Loren (who loves the maniacal Dark Mistress).

Charlotte snobbishly claims to dislikes the series.

Florence is of the opinion that no woman, gay or straight, can resist Roddick’s allure. She believes Charlotte is just being obstinate. The author of the Scarlet Knight is reclusive and uses a pseudonym, corresponds only through her publisher, but is definitely a woman.

# Domremy, Skaag, Skaagi, massacre, Aayden, Aayden's

Domremy was a moderately sized town along Flora's southern coast, in the county of Amaran. Now, however, it is a solemn monument to the tragedy that occurred there a little over 20 years ago. Still in the midst of expanding their navy after the Skaagi conquest of Pradesh, the Florans were unprepared for a lightning fast landing of Skaagi troops at the strategically unremarkable coastal town. The invasion was not intended to secure a foothold, however, but to send a message to all of the Nisran continent: Surrender or face horrific oblivion.

The massacre of the helpless town was truly nightmarish. In ritualistic fashion, the Skaagi piled a mountains of skulls under a shrine they constructed to their war god, violated young women before throwing them into the fires, and impaled the soldiers in ceremonial display. 

The local ruling family were either tortured to death or taken as sexual slaves to serve as trophies for the architect of this Hell, the warlord ruling over Pradesh. The only one 'spared' was the father, who had one eye blinded and crippled, before being left behind to tell the tale of the price of resistance.

The massacre had profound effects on Floran culture, sparking the rapid rise of the imperialist political faction, formerly a minor party. Flora militarized rapidly, became aggressive in their politics, began seeking power over neighboring Duran, anything to accrue enough power to stave off the threat from Skaag's empire of evil. Female supremacists hold this as exhibit A of the barbarity of a society entirely dominated by males.

There was one more tragedy that would follow. The oldest prince of Flora, Aayden, was enraged by this pointless savagery and called for retaliatory crusade to purge the Skaag and liberate Pradesh. Thousands of volunteers rallied to his side from every county, and judging that he couldn't give the Skaag time to reinforce their numbers, rejected the Matriarchs' pleading for delay so they could fully support the invasion. His zealous forces burned Skaag's anchored invasion fleet, landed and devastated their army staging grounds. However, by then, an endless army of Pradeshi conscripts surrounded the Floran invaders.

There could be no victory, only retreat. The Florans fought their way back to their ships and, at nearly a quarter of its former strength, fled back to Flora, where they faced disgrace and condemnation. Domremy's tragedy had been compounded by an apparent military catastrophe, humiliating Flora for a generation. This event became known as Aayden's Folly. 

However, many strategists are beginning to believe that this event actually blunted Skaag's invasion of the continent and gave the Floran Navy the time it needed to establish naval superiority in the Sea of Pradesh. Ironically, amongst the pradeshi people and even the Skaag, Aayden is honored and revered for his daring and courage.
 
Aayden himself died from his wounds before he could return to Flora, his role as the architect of Flora's most humiliating defeat made him a reviled and mocked figure; particularly this was used by a few prominent matriarchs to bolster belief in female supremacy. This further evidence of male folly only confirmed in many minds that men were unfit to lead a civilized nation. It also solidified the rule of the matriarchs, some of whom had very discreetly sabotaged his efforts and capitalized on his death and failure afterwards.

Cornelia, figurehead ruler of Flora and Aayden's younger sister, loathes all those who had a hand in her beloved brother's downfall, and seeks to accrue enough power to finish his work in Pradesh and avenge his memory.

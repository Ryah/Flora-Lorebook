# 134 - The Scattered Mirror Breakthrough
On Thursday, June 22, 1843, at approximately 7:07 AM in the Solarium of the Palace of Loth Logan, Ryan Adame threw water from the copper basin into the air and instructed Charlotte Claymore to catch and freeze multiple droplets simultaneously using a broadcast command rather than individual negotiation. With fifteen loops active—her highest count yet—Charlotte closed her eyes and reached for the water. She caught three droplets, which froze mid-fall as tiny silver-luminescent ice spheres hovering two feet from her hands. The rest splashed to the floor. Charlotte reported feeling dozens of catches but being unable to hold attention on all of them simultaneously. Estra Hamilton identified the core problem: Charlotte was negotiating with individual droplets rather than broadcasting one command to many receivers. Althena reframed the failure as an attention issue—the water is not refusing, it is simply not all paying attention yet. Claire Whitehill contributed the metaphor of calling a room full of people, where some turn around immediately and others need repeated calls.

At 7:08 AM, Ryan demonstrated the broadcast technique by pulling all spilled water back into the basin using only two loops—one command directed at the water as a single entity. Charlotte felt the difference through the sensory bond and articulated the breakthrough: Ryan asked the water as one thing, not thirty separate droplets. Cora documented this as a paradigm shift from parallel negotiation to single broadcast targeting water itself as the receiver. Charlotte's three ice droplets remained hovering throughout, proof of concept sustained.

At 7:09 AM, Charlotte attempted the broadcast approach on her second try. Ryan threw the basin's contents high across the solarium. Charlotte did not close her eyes. She raised her hand and stated her intention—"Freeze"—as a command rather than a question. Eleven droplets crystallized simultaneously, scattered across the solarium at various heights and distances rather than clustered near her hand. Estra noted this confirmed area-effect field rather than targeted catching. Mina identified the combat implication: quick freeze-and-release rather than sustained barriers. However, maintaining eleven separate ice droplets produced visible strain after approximately fifteen seconds—Charlotte's arm trembled, sweat traced her temple, and her breathing grew heavier. Cora documented that maintenance cost scales linearly with target count. Charlotte released the droplets, which melted in place. She acknowledged the broadcast works but approximately sixty-five percent of the water still did not respond, requiring further repetition to learn her voice.

At 7:10 AM, Ryan teased Charlotte about channeling her Countess authority over the droplets or her authority over her spouses in bed. Florence immediately seized the teasing opportunity, forcing Charlotte into flustered deflection. Althena expressed ancient amusement at mortal flirting. Estra redirected discussion to Ryan's observation about glow localization: the silver shimmer manifests at the anatomical point where Ryan mentally wraps the thread. Ankle-wrapping was proposed as optimal concealment since ankles are covered by current fashion. Cora documented secondary concealment options including lower back, upper thigh, and sole of foot. Claire asked whether the power would feel different at the ankle versus the wrist. Ryan released the fifteen loops but secretly kept two active instead of the usual one. Charlotte noticed immediately, catching his test with sharp curiosity rather than anger, demonstrating she can distinguish between loop counts even at low thresholds.

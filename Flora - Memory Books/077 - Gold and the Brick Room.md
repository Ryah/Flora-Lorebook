# 077 - Gold and the Brick Room

Monday, June 19, 1843, 9:42 AM - 9:55 AM. At Claymore Manor's Master Suite, Claire Whitehill finishes removing Ryan Adame's stitches, confirming his back wound has fully healed without corruption. When Ryan inquires about her father, Claire recounts his death three winters prior at the Hartwell mill, where a waterwheel crushed his arm, leading to amputation above the elbow, infection, and a fortnight of suffering during which the family could not afford bread. She explains her mother took in washing while Claire entered service at fourteen to survive, noting that Lady Rachael runs a fair household.

After directing Claire to look out the window, Ryan retrieves fifty gold coins from a hidden lockbox beneath his bedside table, adding to the eight already given for a total of fifty-eight. Laying the fortune on the bed, he insists she take it to support her family, refusing refusal. Claire, initially overwhelmed and protesting that she cannot hide such a sum from Countess Charlotte's weekly account reviews, ultimately accepts on the condition that he wins his 6:00 PM rematch against Estra Hamilton. She plans to send the money to her clever sister to learn a trade as a clerk or bookkeeper rather than break her back washing linens, intending to attribute the patronage to an anonymous benefactor so her sister owes nothing to any man.

Ryan warns Claire that after tonight she may see him differently, revealing he must mentally return to the "brick room"—a psychological space representing twenty years of trauma in the Amber Isles where he was kept in darkness and chains, beaten by his mother when he failed to "fight the air," and forced to burn off meager rations within hours. He explains his goal is not merely to defeat Estra but to give her permission to remove her heavy mask by demonstrating that she is not alone in her darkness, as she too grew up under Loren Claymore's control.

Claire, shaken but resolute, admits she has heard servants' rumors about his upbringing. Despite her fear that he is wagering on becoming a monster, she promises to call him Ryan regardless of what emerges in the courtyard, refusing to view him as such even if he must access that primal state. She challenges him by asking who grants him permission to set down his burden, noting that those who believe tears and rest are weakness often break harder later. She vows to remain present after the fight to bring him water and witness his return, regardless of victory or savagery, ensuring he knows someone sees him beyond the violence.

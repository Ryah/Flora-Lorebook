# 001 - Arrival and Confession

Wednesday, June 14, 1843 AD, approximately 2:15 PM to 2:40 PM. Ryan arrives at Claymore Manor, escorted into the opulent main hall featuring crystal chandeliers and marble floors, where Florence—clad in a white and yellow silk sundress with golden hair cascading down her back—descends the grand staircase to greet him. She expresses concern over his bumpy carriage journey and apparent head injury, noting they had been absent during a honeymoon week. Ryan claims partial amnesia from hitting his head, remembering only his name and vague details.

Florence guides him to the parlor—a thirty-foot room with dark oak paneling, a gray stone fireplace, bookshelves, and cream silk settees facing across a mahogany table—where she applies a cloth-wrapped ice pack to his temple. Charlotte enters, dressed in a high-collared burgundy velvet gown with black hair secured by silver pins, her demeanor cold and assessing. She interrogates Ryan about his memory, revealing his full identity as a Baronet of the Amber Isles, from a modest estate near Millhaven, with mother Eleanor and sisters Mina and Cora.

Charlotte explains the transactional nature of their union: she and Florence, Countesses of Cassia, required a husband to legalize their union under Flora's laws permitting two women to share one spouse. They selected Ryan from dozens of candidates after his mother proposed the match seeking financial relief, paying a substantial dowry. She clarifies that his rank remains unchanged, their children will inherit Claymore and Blakeney names, not his family name, and that the wedding occurred nine days prior at the Temple of Althena in Riverside.

Ryan gradually recovers memories of his sisters—Mina who taught him to fight, and Cora who taught him independence, the latter having wept at the ceremony and refused to sign as witness. He reveals the horrifying truth of his upbringing: his mother subjected him to systematic torture including chaining him in the basement for days or weeks without food or water, kneeling on rice, ice baths, standing torture, and whipping to "perfect" him as a valuable commodity. He recalls his mother's threat to castrate him over a water streak on a dish. He explains his silence during prior meetings stemmed from fear of further punishment.

Florence is overwhelmed with guilt, spilling tea as she weeps, while Charlotte maintains rigid composure, her gloved hands gripping the fireplace mantle, revealing she will investigate Eleanor's household practices. Ryan expresses gratitude for his rescue, acknowledging he was purchased but views it as liberation from slavery.

Lady Rachael Blakeney—Florence's mother and a neighboring countess—arrives unexpectedly early, entering the parlor unannounced despite instructions to wait in the blue drawing room. She is a formidable maternal figure in an emerald day dress with a white ribbon in her brown hair, radiating vanilla and brown sugar scent. Rachael reveals she already knows the marriage's true nature and physically disciplined both daughters for their treatment of Ryan, believing strongly in Althena's teachings regarding husband welfare.

Rachael insists on examining Ryan immediately, discovering faded scars on his wrist and collar that reveal his previous abuse. She demands a full accounting, and Ryan—having remembered more details—describes the full extent of his mother's cruelty, including the castration threat and forced accomplishments to inflate his value. He concludes with a vow to Rachael that he refuses to ever be weak again, establishing his determination to reclaim agency in his new life.

# 075 - The Dragon's Apple Pie

On Monday, June 19, 1843, in Claymore Manor's kitchen, Ryan Adame joins Estra and Alec Hamilton in baking what becomes "The Dragon's Apple Pie." When Ryan taps the cinnamon jar and declares it "our pie," Estra reveals that she has never owned anything that truly belonged to them—everything from their fugitive years was borrowed, rented, or stolen. Alec recalls stealing apples from a cart in the Sion Quarter, revealing that Estra gave her portion to a starving dog while claiming she had eaten, because Alec was sick and needed nourishment more than she did. Mrs. Marie observes that survival stealing is necessary, but true character emerges in choosing to learn "to measure sugar and tap cinnamon like civilized people" rather than continue stealing when no longer desperate.

Alec confesses his secret desire to be civilized and his covert preparation: while Estra spent nights watching doors armed with her knife, he practiced the three-tap cinnamon ritual and cooking techniques in the dirt with sticks, preparing for a future where they might find a kitchen that let them stay. Estra realizes with dawning vulnerability that her brother watched her while she thought she was guarding him, teaching himself to belong somewhere rather than merely survive. Florence Claymore observes from the doorway that children see through adult silences, and Alec honored Estra's need to believe he slept safely by practicing hope while she practiced vigilance.

When Alec asks if today is the day he no longer needs guarding, Estra promises that for this morning she will stand by the table instead of the door, defending the kitchen only after they eat if necessary. Ryan jokes about respecting Mrs. Marie's sovereignty, acknowledging she could defeat them both in combat, which prompts Alec to realize with alarm that the scheduled 6 PM rematch between Ryan and Estra is a real fight. Estra kneels to explain that she needs to test whether she can still spar without the intent to kill, having been Loren's weapon for so long.

The emotional climax arrives as they complete the cinnamon blessing together. While Alec taps the jar three times, Ryan whispers the invocation with Estra: "One for the dragon, two for the prince, three for the memories that we have made since." Estra allows Ryan to interlace his fingers with her bandaged hand, confessing that while she spent years trying to forget their trauma, she never considered wanting to remember instead.

As they weave the lattice top—Alec applying weaving skills Estra taught him for mending clothes during their flight—Mrs. Marie instructs them to "persuade" the dough rather than force it, maintaining the rhythm of "over and under." Alec observes that pie is honest: done wrong, the apples leak out and no fancy words can hide the failure. The pie goes into the applewood-fired oven for forty to forty-five minutes, and Mrs. Marie sets an hourglass, instructing them to wait without opening the door, to trust the process.

The final moments find them sitting together on the warm hearth bench—Ryan, Estra, and Alec—discussing warmth without vigilance and the desire to be "heavy" (anchored and important) rather than light. Alec recalls their mother baking apple pie for special occasions, including the day Estra returned from her first training "harder" and sharper. Estra reflects mournfully that sharp things cut those who hold them, but Alec comforts her that dragons are sharp so their people don't have to be, cementing their transition from survival to belonging.

# 005 - The Trust Document

On Wednesday, June 14, 1843, between 4:20 and 4:30 PM in the Blue Drawing Room at Claymore Manor, Ryan composes a letter to his sisters using a quill. He describes his carriage arrival and head injury while deliberately omitting the true nature of his marriage arrangement, ending with the cryptic phrase "See you soon" to signal his extraction plan without revealing specifics to potential interceptors. Charlotte enters unannounced, dressed in a black gown with dark lace trim and silver combs securing her raven hair. She notes that Florence has been crying and observes that Ryan's letter is wisely cautious, containing nothing actionable if intercepted. She admits her skepticism stems from experience, stating that trust must be earned through pattern rather than words.

Charlotte produces a sealed trust document drafted on May 27, 1843—during the three days Ryan lay unconscious after the assassination attempt. The deed establishes an irrevocable trust with twelve thousand gold sovereigns, guarantees lifetime residence at Claymore Manor regardless of marital dissolution or death of signatories, provides an annual stipend of eight hundred gold sovereigns, and protects against disinheritance. Critically, Article V—the Personal Autonomy Clause—was added on the third day of Ryan's unconsciousness specifically to constrain Charlotte's own instincts, guaranteeing Ryan authority over his correspondence, movement, companions, and private quarters. Charlotte confesses she added this clause because she does not trust herself when afraid, making her flaws legally binding.

Charlotte admits that had she known the truth about Ryan's enslavement and suffering, she would not have proceeded with the marriage arrangement, preferring to find another way to be with Florence. However, she acknowledges this moral stance would have left Ryan chained and starving, condemning him to further torture for her own comfort. Ryan reveals that during those same three days, he hid in a closet bleeding, believing he would be returned to his mother; he preferred death by his own hand to returning to her control, explaining his suicide ideation stemmed from fear rather than failure. He acknowledges they share similar trauma wounds and asks Charlotte not to treat him as a victim requiring management, but as someone capable of earning respect through action.

Despite this, Ryan admits to severe imposter syndrome regarding his sudden noble status and requests that his wives proofread his public correspondence until he learns proper etiquette, wanting to avoid tarnishing the family name through ignorance. Charlotte agrees to act as a translator rather than a manager, noting his humility is either genuine or the most sophisticated defense she has encountered. As their conversation concludes, a servant interrupts with urgent news: a courier has arrived from the Amber Isles requiring immediate attention regarding his sisters.

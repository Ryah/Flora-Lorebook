# 069 - Variables and Tempering

On Sunday, June 18, 1843, in the sun-warmed Eastern Courtyard of Claymore Manor, Ryan Adame confides in his sister Mina about his upcoming rematch with Estra Hamilton. He insists on fifty-fifty odds, clarifying he does not intend to lose but seeks the raw emotional honesty of combat—the unadulterated adrenaline that strips away the noble mask he must wear, reminiscent yet distinct from their sparring sessions in captivity. Mina, appearing in a sweat-dampened undershirt with scarred forearms and gray eyes filled with sisterly concern, warns that Estra fights for survival and respect, not recreation, and will not pull punches. Ryan reveals he has obsessively analyzed Estra's weaknesses—slower rotation to the right from years of sleeping posture, a weaker left arm, and telltale hesitation when analyzing moves—but intends to use this knowledge to help her prove she belongs, not to destroy her. He displays his healed hand from their blood ritual wedding, noting how their dynamic has shifted from kidnapped prince and captor to willing partners bound by choice.

Ryan then recounts the morning's trauma processing: Estra had been paranoid and sleepless, convinced Loren would take Alec, unable to rest without Ryan present. To help her process this rage, Ryan drew a crude likeness of Loren's face on paper, nailed it to a training dummy in the courtyard, and instructed Estra to kill it. He stood witness as she unleashed feral aggression, repeatedly asking permission to continue until the maple sap flowed like blood, providing catharsis through controlled violence while Howard, Charlotte, and Florence observed from a distance. When Estra finally collapsed from exhaustion, Ryan caught her and carried her to bed. Howard later warned Ryan that he had "shattered her" and now bore responsibility for rebuilding her.

The conversation shifts to Ryan's psychological state as he acknowledges overextending himself to prove worth to his new family. He confesses severe imposter syndrome regarding his titles—Lord Claymore and Prince Ryan—feeling he must earn his place despite having rescued his sisters, Alec, and fundamentally altered the household dynamics. Mina challenges this mentality, insisting that family is not transactional and that he became worthy the moment he chose to stay and love them rather than flee to safety. She accepts his gratitude for being the "variables" who broke Loren's mathematical cruelty by teaching him humanity when they were supposed to treat him as a manufactured project.

Ryan reveals the depth of his fear during their extraction from Eleanor's house, admitting he was willing to let himself be recaptured if it ensured Mina and Cora's escape, trusting Howard and Rachael would eventually rescue him. Mina forcefully rejects this sacrifice mentality, declaring he is no longer disposable and that she did not fight to free him only to watch him volunteer for chains again. 

Finally, Mina shares crucial intelligence about Eleanor's connection to Loren. She recalls hearing Eleanor whisper to someone codenamed "L" about Ryan's "conditioning" and "progress," treating him as a long-term investment. Mina theorizes that Loren deliberately cultivated Eleanor—selecting a woman desperate for status and legitimacy—to temper Ryan like steel through abuse: keeping him alive just enough to become hardened yet psychologically dependent, a weapon forged over years that Loren is now coming to collect. She concludes that Eleanor was never the architect but the furnace, and Ryan was always meant to be retrieved once properly tempered.

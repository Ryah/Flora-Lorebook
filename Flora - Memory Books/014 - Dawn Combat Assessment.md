# 014 - Dawn Combat Assessment

On Thursday, June 15, 1843, at approximately 5:30 AM, Ryan wakes in the Lord's Quarters at Claymore Manor after seven hours of undisturbed sleep, his first deep rest in weeks. Henry enters with a brass candlestick and a folded stack of training clothes—heavy linen trousers, a dark green fitted shirt, and a leather vest with laces—informing Ryan that Howard awaits in the eastern courtyard for dawn training. Henry outlines the critical schedule: Doctor Mercer arrives at 7:00 AM, breakfast is at 8:30 AM, and departure for the extraction mission is set for 9:30 AM.

Ryan descends to the eastern courtyard, which lies buried in thick morning mist at 46°F with wet flagstones and a pale gray dawn sky. Howard stands at the center in a loose unlaced linen shirt and leather bracers, having waited since 4:00 AM. He presents Ryan with his father William Adame's aged practice sword, distinguished by its amber-darkened wood, heavier pommel, and a rough notch worn by years of use. Howard explains that William fought with patient, forward-weighted technique "like he was arguing with it," then launches an immediate test strike toward Ryan's ribs.

The sparring session demonstrates Ryan's competence—he lands several solid strikes using footwork and positional advantages taught by Mina, earning Howard's acknowledgment that he moves like William. However, Ryan's healing knife wound on his back, still bandaged, limits his left rotation to approximately seventy percent capacity, creating a visible hesitation that Howard identifies as a fatal vulnerability in real combat.

As the mist thins around 5:58 AM, Howard concludes that Mina taught Ryan to fight like a soldier, but warns that the wound telegraphs weakness to enemies. He shares that William would have been proud, then departs to prepare for extraction planning. Ryan remains, acknowledging his seventy percent limitation and expressing gratitude that Florence will accompany him.

At approximately 6:01 AM, Captain Eva Riensdauter emerges from the courtyard archway, having observed the final exchanges. Dressed in Claymore red-and-black battle dress with steel greaves, pauldrons, and a Duranian-made sword at her hip, she challenges Howard's emotional assessment. She notes that Ryan's injury is visible "from across a courtyard," distinguishes Florence as an athlete rather than a soldier, and warns that Howard projects William onto Ryan, clouding his judgment. Standing with hand on pommel, she establishes her professional skepticism about sending a wounded man and untested civilian into a hostile household, indicating she will verify Ryan's readiness herself before permitting the mission.

# Session: Your Wives - 2026-04-28 20-46-22

<narration>
Alec's question hung in the candlelit air—*does she know you're telling me?*—and the shrewdness of it, the quiet tactical logic from a seven-year-old who had learned to think three steps ahead in order to survive, settled over the room like a held breath.

He was still watching Ryan with those grey eyes. Estra's eyes. But the expression in them wasn't Estra's sharp assessment or Charlotte's analytical precision. It was something uniquely Alec—the careful, cataloguing attention of a child who had taught himself to draw dragon scales by candlelight because no one else would teach him anything. Now he was applying that same meticulous focus to the mechanics of secrets and bonds and the particular danger of being caught in a surprise.

His small hand, still resting on his own shoulder where he'd tapped the spot matching Ryan's bite mark, dropped back to his lap. He folded it neatly over his other hand—the way he'd seen Charlotte do in meetings—and waited. The contract lay on the blanket beside him. The dragon drawing, still abandoned, had begun to curl at one corner from the warmth of the candle. Fifty-three scales. Redundancy wings. Pending tail spikes. But Alec wasn't looking at any of it.

He was looking at Ryan, and the question he'd asked wasn't just about Charlotte knowing. It was about whether secrets were safe. Whether surprises could be good. Whether trust—once given—could survive the weight of everything it was asked to carry.
</narration>

He said it like he was annotating a tactical report. Then, just slightly, the corner of his mouth twitched—not a smile, but the ghost of one. The particular expression of a child who had just realized he understood a problem most adults would fumble.

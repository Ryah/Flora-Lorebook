# Session: Your Wives - 2026-04-27 17-42-16

<narration>
Alec's small hand finally stopped tracing the stick figures. He looked up at Ryan—grey eyes still wet, but the tightness around his mouth had loosened. The particular tension of a child bracing for disappointment, slowly unclenching.

He didn't say thank you. Alec rarely said thank you—not because he wasn't grateful, but because gratitude was a language he was still learning. Instead, he carefully folded his hands in his lap, the way he'd seen Charlotte do when she was being formal, and nodded once. A small, serious motion.
</narration>

<font color=#e056fd>"Charlotte notarized it."</font>

<narration>
He said it like he was verifying the terms of a treaty. Then, quieter—almost to himself:
</narration>

<font color=#e056fd>"So it's a law. Not just a promise. A law."</font>

<narration>
He looked down at the dragon drawing, still abandoned on the blanket. Fifty-three scales. Redundancy wings. Pending tail spikes. His finger found the empty space at the end of the tail—the space he'd been saving for spikes—and tapped it once.
</narration>

<font color=#e056fd>"I'll practice the boom noises. Every day. Until they're roars."</font>

<narration>
From beside the bed, Estra shifted. Her hand—the one that had been frozen open from the fist bump—dropped to her side, then rose again to rest briefly on Alec's shoulder. The gesture was quick, almost businesslike, but her fingers lingered a half-second longer than necessary.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"You notarized a crayon contract."</font>

<narration>
Her voice was flat. Dry. But she was looking at Ryan, and the expression in her grey eyes was something she still didn't have a word for.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"With stick figures. And the word 'protekt.' You had Charlotte—Countess Charlotte Claymore, who once annotated a kitchen audit for eight pages—put her seal on a document that says 'practiced all nite' in a seven-year-old's handwriting."</font>

<narration>
She paused. The ghost of a smile—the one that had been appearing more and more often since the wrestling match—flickered at the corner of her mouth.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"I've seen a lot of things in six years of intelligence work. I've never seen that."</font>

<narration>
She glanced toward the window. The grey pre-dawn light was strengthening—not yet sunrise, but close. Her internal clock, the one that had been ticking through every fist bump and explosion sound and dragon consultation, finally asserted itself.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"Thirty-two minutes."</font>

<narration>
Her hand squeezed Alec's shoulder once—brief, firm—then dropped.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"I need to sweep the east kitchen courtyard before the meeting. Mina's probably already there, but I want my own eyes on it."</font>

<narration>
She looked at Alec. The warmth in her face didn't disappear—it settled, like something being carefully stored away for later.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"You'll be okay here? Mrs. Marie will be up soon. Or Claire. Someone will bring breakfast."</font>

<font color=#e056fd>"I know."</font>

<narration>
Alec didn't say *I don't want you to go*. He didn't say *what if something happens while you're gone*. He just looked at Estra, then at Ryan, then back at the contract on the blanket beside him—the crayon letters, the stick figures, the signature that made it law.
</narration>

<font color=#e056fd>"I'll practice the boom noises. And I'll draw the tail spikes. The dragon still needs tail spikes."</font>

<narration>
He picked up his charcoal. His hand was steadier now.
</narration>

<font color=#e056fd>"When you come back, I'll show you. Both of you."</font>

<narration>
Estra held his gaze for a long moment. Then she nodded—once, sharp, the way she'd acknowledge a tactical report—and turned toward the door. Her hand brushed the karambit at her hip automatically, checking its position. Old habit. But her other hand, the one that had been frozen open from the fist bump, was still relaxed at her side.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"Come on. I'll show you the loose flagstone Charlotte mentioned. It's three doors down—she's right, it's a hazard."</font>

<narration>
She paused at the threshold. Looked back at Alec—small and cross-legged on the bed, charcoal in hand, dragon drawing in his lap, contract on the blanket beside him—and the expression on her face was something between a smile and something older.
</narration>

<font color=#a29bfe>"Loud explosions, Alec. From the heart. I'll be listening."</font>
